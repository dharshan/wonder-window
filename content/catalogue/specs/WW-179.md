# WW-179 · Through Their Eyes (our id `ww179`)
Status: **design_spec_draft** (awaiting father/reviewer approval) · Category: G. Spatial Awareness & Visual Intelligence / Perspective · Variant: `pick`

**1. Identity.** Original title "Turn World", catalogue id WW-179. Source record unchanged in master.json.

**2. Age band.** Record 6-8 (WW-191 for 3-4); L1 (who likes what) and L2 (where will it look?) fit him at 5.5; L3 is a stretch.

**3. Objective (observable).** He works out how another person or animal sees, knows or feels something different from himself: what each one likes (L1), what a friend believes when it did not see the object move (L2), who knows, who sees more, who feels differently (L3).

**4. What he practises.** Perspective taking and early theory of mind (my view is not the only view); listening to a short story and remembering who saw what; fairness to differing preferences. (Source objective: "Understand simple different viewpoints".)

**5. Originality and dedup.** Emotion Stories (ww029) and Feeling Faces name a feeling for a situation; Kind Choice (ww010) chooses a kind reply. None asks what someone else knows or believes. Same pick action, new objective. Cluster WW-DUP-037 (WW-179, WW-191): WW-191 is aliased here. WW-168 (how another feels or thinks) went to Emotion Stories. Decision: **own game**.

**6. Core loop.** Two animals sit on the board with what matters (what they like; a hidden object and two places; who peeked). A short story is spoken on 🔊, then he picks who or where. The correct answer is always from the other one's point of view, never his own.
Example rounds:
- L1 `ww179-1-01`: "What does it like?", board 🐱 🐟 🐄 🌿, choices 🌿 🌸 🐟, answer 🐟. Spoken: "The cat likes fish. The cow likes grass. What does the cat like?"
- L2 `ww179-2-01`: "Where will it look first?", board 🐱 ⚽ 📦 ➡️ 🧺, choices 📦 🧺 🛏️, answer 📦. Spoken: "The cat puts the ball in the box and goes out. While it is away, the dog moves the ball to the basket. The cat comes back. Where will the cat look first?"
- L3 `ww179-3-01`: "Who knows? Who sees?", board 📦 🐱 👀 🐶, choices 🐶 🐱, answer 🐱. Spoken: "The cat peeked inside the closed box and saw a fish. The dog did not peek. Who knows what is inside?"

**7. Child-facing instruction.** Visual demo by Cherry in round 1 of the first session. Narration only on 🔊: "Cherry's friends are all different. They like different things, and know different things. Can you see it their way?" Prompt text 8 words or fewer (he can't read; it is for the parent).

**8. Remote mapping.** LEFT/RIGHT over the choices, OK confirms, UP 🔊, BACK pause. Initial focus: 🔊 (pulsing) on the first round, then the first choice. Focus never lands on an inert element; exactly one element is focused.

**9. Difficulty.** L1 two characters with a favourite each, he picks what one likes (answers can be seen on the board). L2 the hidden-object story (one wrong answer is where the object really is). L3 who peeked, who sits high, who is happy or bothered. Levels: L1 What they like 💛; L2 What they know 🙈; L3 Through their eyes 👀.

**10. Hint / retry / recovery.** After 1 miss the choice fades and the `hint` is shown (voice only on 🔊). After 2 misses the answer is shown and he presses it ("You kept going!"). 🔊 always replays the line. Nothing is lost.

**11. Success, session end, early exit.** Session = 5 rounds, then the end_lines and the Play again / Back to games buttons. Early exit any time via BACK: it cancels any local action first, then opens the pause sheet; a second BACK returns to the host. No confirmation is needed because no progress is lost.

**12. Feedback.** Matches the record ("Gentle, positive visual feedback on success. Neutral and constructive response to errors with opportunity to try again. No pressure, timers, or negative messaging."). Correct: one of 6 `cheers`, the scene fills (👀), soft `correct` sound. Miss: neutral wobble plus `tryagain` taps; never red, never "wrong".

**13. Session time.** About 20-35 s per round; 5 rounds take about 2-3 min. Target 1.5-4 min (record estimate 1.5-4 min).

**14. Assets / audio / content.** Animal and object emoji; arrow between two places; no names of real people. All spoken lines are pre-recorded clips (Rishi), with voice only after 🔊. Content: `catalogue/catalogue_games.json` → `ww179` (30 rounds, 3 levels).

**15. Accessibility.** Meaning is carried by the pictures, not by colour alone; labels are screen text for the parent only. Characters are animals, never real people; no frightening scenes (the afraid-of-dogs round shows an emoji face and no scary dog). Plus: focus ring #F59E0B 8 px plus glow, targets at least 240 px, reduced-motion flag honoured, all audio optional, no reading required.

**16. Offline / failure.** Fully bundled; no network. If content fails to load or validate, the card is hidden and an error screen returns to the host.

**17. Parent options / data.** Enable/disable, starting level, reset. Stored locally: current level per game only. Reset clears it without touching time settings.

**18. Tests.** Validator: `validate_games.py` (schema, answer keys, 8-word prompts, Unicode 11 emoji, TTS rules). Each round has one defensible answer (plus `also_ok` where listed); choices distinct; labels match choices; `fade` is never a right answer. Plus host contract tests: launch blocked when disabled or out of budget; budget expiry in instructions, play, feedback and pause leads to a calm end; BACK in every state; cold start in airplane mode; a real-remote pass on the OnePlus TV.

**19. Evidence and limits.** Background: false-belief tasks (Wellman, Cross & Watson, 2001, Child Development meta-analysis) show that many children pass them between 4 and 6. Limitation: this is task practice, not a claim of better social skill. We claim only practice of the skill inside the activity.
